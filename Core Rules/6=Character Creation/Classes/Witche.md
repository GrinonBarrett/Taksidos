# Witche
Reclusive individuals that practice meditation, hermitism, and unorthodox practices. The witche has managed to align itself with another way of life which has manifested itself in the ability to concoct unusual things that defy traditional alchemy. These folks often walk a fine line between being important, if unusual, members of their little world and being reviled pariahs.

### Fringe Knowledge
Choose one of the following skills (Alchemy, Cooking, Fabrication, Lore, Medicine, Spellcraft, Survival, Volierence), you gain competency in it.  
Additionally, anytime you perform a check with one of those listed skills, regardless of your competency with the skill, you may choose to roll a d20. If you do, magical effects cannot alter the result of the check itself or the roll and a natural result of 20 grants a +2 bonus to the result.

### Preperations
You have learned how to produce concoctions capable of absorbing magical powers. You are capable of learning recipes for how to produce such preperations as though they were spells. The time to learn a recipe is half the time to learn a spell. 

You may produce a powder, potion, oil or poultice as appropriate. This requires an amount of time equal to the casting time multiplied by the spell tier squared (treat a reaction as a single action). It also requires materials which cost as much as the spell tier squared. A powder is shelf stable and is ruined by being dampened or otherwise destroyed or defused. An oil has a shelf life of six months. A poultice is immediately activated upon its creation. A potion can last 4d6+heroism days in traditional containers- but this is increased to 8d8+level+heroism days if stored in glass.  
When producing the product, you perform a spellcheck- the result of which is known only to the narrator. If you know the spell which the recipe is trying to capture, you gain a 1d4 bonus. The product will also require time to set- a number of hours equal to the spell tier squared minus your level. If this would be 0 or less, it is instead an hour divided by your level. A poultice ignores this (see below). You also gain spell sickness equal to the spell's tier when you finish the product. The production of a preparation cannot be counterspelled, however a creature that knows the spell and perceives the product will know its nature.

Your preparations only works if used by you, though you could offer a potion to a creature which can imbibe it before the end of the next round. The medium you produced must be used to either cover (or be imbibed by) the subject (if it targets/affects creatures/objects) or the area if the spell affects an area. These preperations cannot perform effects that produce projectiles (as part of an attack) or ray attacks, but can affect or create objects or inflict other attacks.

The use of such a product is a single action, regardless of the spell's casting time. If the spell had a casting time of a reaction, it can be used in that way.

You know the recipe for one basic spell of your choice, a basic spell of your choice with a minimum tier of 1, and another spell of the narrator's choice.

### 2) Wary
For saves against magical effects, you replace your heroism bonus with your level.  
When you perform a save against a magical effect, you may use your reaction to gain a bonus equal to your remaining hitpoints.

### 2) Weird Impulse
Anytime another creature you can perceive performs a check or save, you may use your reaction to either *increase or decrease that creature's result for the purpose of determening whether it is a critical success or critical failure equal to your appropriate attribute bonus. If you do, you suffer spell sickness equal to this modification -1 (minimum of 1 spell sickness)*.

### 3) Familiarity in the Art
The time to learn new recipes is now the time to learn a spell divided by your heroism.

### 3) Strange Brew
If another's preperation would affect you, and you know the recipe for that preperation or the associated spell- you gain a bonus to defense and saves against it equal to your level.  

### 4) Scar the Soul
Anytime one of your spells would qualify to damage armor against a foe you may choose instead to have it inflict Archeblight. If the attack would have had an alternate effect when it qualified to damage armor, this will override that modification.  
When you produce a preperation, you may choose whether this is to be applied to it or not.

### 6) Crooked Gaze
Once per encounter, you may cast your gaze at a foe which can perceive you. So long as you concentrate on this gaze, *that foe's attacks against you have disadvantage so long as it can perceive you*. Additionally, *it has disadvantage on saves against fear effects that originate from you*.

### 7) Heretical Workings
When your counterspell check against a creature is successful, you may choose not to increase the casting time by half. If you do, it will instead cause the spell to also apply the effect that would have occurred upon a critically failed spellcheck when the spellcheck is resolved (in addition to the effect of that spellcheck resolving). It will use their points of failure on the counterspell check for this purpose. If your counterspell check result was even, they will be made aware of this effect.

### 8) Harrowing
When you attack a creature with spell sickness, if the total result of your attack equals their spell sickness, *the attack qualifies to damage armor*.  
If a creature affected by your Crooked Gaze dies or is otherwise incapacitated, you may affect a new creature in the same encounter.

### 9) Cruel Endurance
Choose one of the following damage types (Ki, Toxic, Necrotic, Radiant). Once this decision is made, it cannot be changed. When an attack of that type against you is a critical failure, *you may use your reaction to attempt to be restored by it. You have a % chance equal to the attack's points of failure. If successful, you recover a hitpoint and receive a point of spell sickness. If the attack result was 0 or less, your chance of success is increased by your heroism. If it is not successful, you recover half of your levels of exhaustion (round up)- up to that creature's missing hitpoints*.  
Anytime you perform an incapacitation save, *you may choose to treat it as a critical success*. You must complete a number of full rests equal to the total number of wounds you possessed to do so again and receive a point of spell sickness.

### Splash or Specialize
At levels 5 and 10, you may choose to splash another class into your training or choose instead to specialize. If you already specialized, you must splash.

#### Specialize
You gain an additional Fringe learning skill.  
You know the result of the spellcheck of your preperations.  
You may salvage material from successful preperations you made to refine the preperation. The new preperation must be of an equal or higher tier. You may choose whether to use the new spellcheck or old. The cost of the new preperation is reduced by half of the one used (or its entirety if it was a critical success).  
You may make preperations for spells you know without having to learn a new recipe. If you learn the recipe, it is considered to have a +1 bonus to the spellcheck.  
The spellcheck of preperations gains a +1 for the purpose of determining critical success based on the number of following skills you are competent in. Furthermore, you may treat your [Magic] attribute as being equal to the number of the following skills and the advanced training bonuses in the following skills you possess (Alchemy, Cooking, Spellcraft, Medicine, Survival).

#### Splash
If you splash witche as a member of another class, you gain the following:  
You are able to produce preparations, but can have no more tiers of spells worth than your heroism produced at a time.  
You also gain competency in a skill listed by the Fringe Knowledge feature. If you already have competency in all of those skills, you instead learn two languages of your choice.

A non-humanoid member of this class may choose to treat any instance of the medicine skill (for the purpose of the above abilities) with the appropriate zoology skill to their species' creature type. If they do, this decision cannot be changed.
